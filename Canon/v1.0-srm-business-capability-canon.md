## 1. Purpose

This Canon freezes the accepted business direction and functional capability baseline for SRM v1.0. Its purpose is to prevent product drift while architecture placement, repository structure, and implementation roadmap are designed in subsequent governed tasks.

This Canon defines what SRM is expected to achieve and which functional capabilities belong to its product direction. It does not define final technical architecture, file placement, deployment topology, implementation sequence, or delivery dates.

Future SRM requirements, architecture decisions, repository allocations, vertical slices, demos, and roadmap items MUST trace back to this Canon or to a later explicitly approved version that supersedes it.

## 2. Technical identity and client context

The canonical technical and product identity is SRM.

PORR is the client and the source of the business discovery material. PORR MUST NOT be used as the technical project name, application namespace, package name, database namespace, PMOS project identity, PHR namespace, deployment identity, or reusable module identity.

Client-specific requirements MAY be represented as configuration, tenant policy, integration mapping, or implementation scope without changing the SRM technical identity.

## 4. Normative interpretation

The keywords MUST, MUST NOT, SHOULD, SHOULD NOT, and MAY are normative.

Capability maturity labels used by this Canon are:

- FROZEN_CORE — required foundation of the SRM product direction;
- FROZEN_DIRECTION — accepted functional direction whose detailed design remains open;
- CONDITIONAL_LATER — accepted as a possible later capability but not required for the first useful product;
- OPEN_DECISION — unresolved business input that MUST NOT be invented by an implementation agent.

The original wishlist is a capability direction. It MUST NOT be interpreted as a frozen one-screen-per-item backlog or as an implementation order.

## 5. Product definition

SRM is a dedicated supplier-management product stream within the wider Profitia and SG2.0 repository ecosystem. It is not a SpendGuru screen and MUST retain separate product ownership, runtime boundaries, routing, configuration, deployment, and persistence wherever separation is required.

SRM is intended first for internal procurement, business, and tender teams. It SHOULD replace fragmented supplier discovery and relationship knowledge currently distributed across web searches, historical email, spreadsheets, documents, forms, SAP extracts, Power Apps prototypes, and individual employee knowledge.

The stable product center is one trusted Supplier Profile. Other capabilities consume, enrich, search, evaluate, or act on that profile.

## 6. Product principles

1. Supplier identity before feature breadth.
2. One Supplier Profile, many bounded capabilities.
3. Controlled dictionaries before uncontrolled free text where classification drives search or ownership.
4. Explicit data source, freshness, and confidence.
5. Human approval before supplier creation, invitation, or consequential status change where business review is required.
6. Governed import before fragile direct integration when an import can deliver the first useful result safely.
7. Reusable modules with clear contracts rather than logic coupled to one screen or shell.
8. Internal usability first; supplier self-service and deep enterprise automation MAY follow later.
9. Business recommendation and actionability over indiscriminate reproduction of raw data.
10. No client name in technical identity.

## 7. Frozen capability map

### SRM-CAP-001 — Supplier Master and Identity

Maturity: FROZEN_CORE

SRM MUST maintain a canonical supplier identity capable of relating at least:

- supplier name and legal name;
- NIP;
- KRS where applicable;
- SAP supplier identifier where available;
- source-system identifiers;
- current and historical addresses;
- branches, sites, and related entities;
- contact people, roles, email addresses, telephone numbers, and website;
- source, freshness, and verification state of material data.

Identity resolution and deduplication across NIP, KRS, SAP ID, and external sources are prerequisites for trustworthy search, history, ratings, reporting, and integration.

### SRM-CAP-002 — Registry, Compliance, Finance, and X-Ray

Maturity: FROZEN_CORE

SRM MUST support a reusable X-Ray capability that can enrich or present a Supplier Profile with external intelligence. The direction includes:

- lookup by NIP or KRS;
- legal and registry data;
- company representation;
- current registered address;
- VAT, CEIDG, or equivalent registry status where relevant;
- beneficial-owner information;
- white-list, black-list, sanction, or other compliance results available from approved providers;
- financial data for all periods available from the provider;
- revenue, costs, profit or loss, and other selected financial indicators;
- concise warnings, status, or recommendation with date and validity where such a conclusion is available and governed;
- source and last-update information for every material data block.

X-Ray MUST be designed as a reusable application module with a clear integration contract. It MUST be consumable by the SRM shell without coupling its business logic to one SRM screen. Literal iframe embedding is not the target architecture.

MGBI and Vercly are current candidate or existing data-provider surfaces. Their exact reuse, ownership, API contract, and canonical source location are subjects of the later architecture-allocation task, not this Canon.

### SRM-CAP-003 — Category Taxonomy and Supplier Coverage

Maturity: FROZEN_CORE

SRM MUST support governed classification of suppliers by:

- procurement categories;
- works, services, or supplied materials;
- detailed specialization;
- territory or region served;
- sites or operating locations;
- category and relationship ownership.

Categories that drive discovery, responsibility, onboarding, or reporting MUST use a controlled dictionary. Free text MAY supplement but MUST NOT replace the governed taxonomy.

The final category dictionary, hierarchy, and category owners remain OPEN_DECISION.

### SRM-CAP-004 — Supplier Discovery, Search, Comparison, and Map

Maturity: FROZEN_DIRECTION

SRM SHOULD provide:

- Google-like supplier search;
- substring and partial-name search;
- filters by category, region, evaluation, ownership, agreement, ADP, and supplier status;
- search for alternative suppliers;
- comparison of selected suppliers;
- geographic presentation where reliable territory or location data exists.

A geographic map is secondary to supplier identity and data quality. It MUST NOT block the first useful discovery experience.

### SRM-CAP-005 — Evaluations, Risk, Red Flags, and ADP

Maturity: FROZEN_DIRECTION

SRM MUST keep separate concepts for:

- supplier performance evaluation;
- procurement-process evaluation;
- red flags and their reasons;
- ADP status, recency, expiry, renewal, and request workflow;
- external compliance and financial-risk signals.

The latest meeting evidence indicates a procurement-process rating scale where 1 is best and 4 is worst. The formal scale, criteria, weights, owners, and aggregation rules remain OPEN_DECISION and MUST be confirmed before implementation as a canonical scoring model.

ADP SHOULD expose at least missing, current, outdated or expired, and renewal-required states. The final workflow MUST use the approved PORR template or a later approved replacement.

### SRM-CAP-006 — Cooperation History, Spend, and Prior Projects

Maturity: FROZEN_DIRECTION

SRM SHOULD associate a supplier with available cooperation history, including:

- turnover by year and period;
- turnover by category and project;
- quantities and unit prices where available and appropriate;
- prior PORR projects;
- prior evaluations;
- related documents and source references;
- data origin and refresh date.

Initial delivery MAY use governed SAP exports, Excel imports, evaluation files, KSeF-derived data, or other approved batch sources. Direct SAP integration is not required for the first useful product.

### SRM-CAP-007 — Agreements and Strategic Relationship Management

Maturity: FROZEN_DIRECTION

SRM SHOULD support:

- framework, partner, or other governed agreement types;
- agreement status, scope, validity period, and documents;
- expiry and renewal alerts;
- designation of strategic suppliers;
- annual relationship or cooperation plans;
- plan-versus-actual monitoring.

The exact prioritization and must-have fields remain subject to roadmap and architecture decisions.

### SRM-CAP-008 — Relationship Ownership and Organizational Knowledge

Maturity: FROZEN_CORE

SRM MUST be able to represent:

- buyer or procurement owner;
- relationship or account owner;
- strategic-supplier owner where applicable;
- people who know or have worked with a supplier;
- local and global responsibility where the organization requires it.

This capability exists to preserve organizational knowledge and reduce dependency on individual memory or private mailboxes.

### SRM-CAP-009 — Meetings, Notes, Tasks, and Interaction History

Maturity: FROZEN_DIRECTION

SRM SHOULD provide:

- meeting and contact chronology;
- participants;
- meeting purpose and outcome;
- notes and attachments;
- next steps, tasks, and follow-ups;
- calendar or scheduled interaction view;
- preparation context or supplier brief where later automation permits it.

The historically named meeting map is canonically interpreted first as a chronology or relationship-history view. A geographic meeting map is optional.

Manual interaction recording is acceptable initially. O365 or Outlook synchronization is CONDITIONAL_LATER.

### SRM-CAP-010 — Supplier Onboarding and Self-Service

Maturity: FROZEN_DIRECTION

SRM SHOULD support a governed onboarding flow that may include:

- supplier registration from a PORR or other tenant-owned entry point;
- validation of NIP, KRS, company identity, and duplicates;
- forms and approved templates;
- anti-bot and abuse protection;
- human approval before invitation or canonical supplier creation;
- later supplier maintenance of selected data.

External self-service is not required before the internal product becomes useful and governed.

### SRM-CAP-011 — Communication Intake

Maturity: CONDITIONAL_LATER

SRM MAY support incoming supplier communication through:

- a forwarding or dedicated address;
- assignment to a supplier profile;
- duplicate detection;
- human classification and acceptance;
- later Outlook, Microsoft 365, Azure, or equivalent automation.

Automatic creation or irreversible classification from incoming mail MUST NOT occur without an approved workflow and deduplication policy.

### SRM-CAP-012 — Supplier Intelligence, News, Alerts, and Briefing

Maturity: FROZEN_DIRECTION

SRM SHOULD support curated external intelligence, including:

- relevant company news;
- material registry, ownership, finance, or compliance changes;
- configurable alerts;
- supplier brief before a meeting;
- concise internal recommendation or status with date and validity.

The product SHOULD prioritize actionable signals over an unfiltered news stream or raw financial-document repository.

### SRM-CAP-013 — Roles, Views, and Personalization

Maturity: FROZEN_DIRECTION

SRM SHOULD support role-appropriate views for procurement, business, tender teams, category owners, relationship owners, and other approved internal users.

The direction includes personal or category dashboards, assigned suppliers, saved views, preferences, and organization-wide versus user-specific information.

The final authorization model remains subject to the architecture task.

### SRM-CAP-014 — Integration, Import, Provenance, and Data Quality

Maturity: FROZEN_CORE

SRM MUST treat integration and data governance as a cross-cutting capability. Candidate sources include SAP, approved Excel or CSV files, existing evaluation systems, KSeF or reports, Power Apps or Dataverse evidence, SharePoint or OneDrive, Outlook, registry providers, MGBI, and Vercly.

For every material integration or import, SRM MUST define:

- source authority;
- identity and join keys;
- refresh method and cadence;
- data ownership;
- validation and deduplication;
- failure and recovery behavior;
- provenance and last-update visibility;
- tenant boundary and access policy.

Power Apps and Dataverse prototypes are evidence and user-interface inspiration. They are not the approved SRM data model or target architecture.

## 8. Cross-cutting product constraints

The following constraints are frozen for SRM unless superseded by a later approved Canon:

### 8.1 Multi-tenancy

The initial tenancy model is:

ORGANIZATION -> USER

Organization-owned data MAY include shared supplier lists, configuration, common saved elements, and organization-specific business data. Organization-plus-user data MAY include assigned suppliers, private watchlists, preferences, and saved views.

Data belonging to one organization MUST NOT be accessible to another organization. Multi-organization membership is not part of v1.0 unless introduced by a later explicit decision.

### 8.2 Languages

Polish is the primary language. English is a full supported language. Internationalization MUST be present from the start rather than added after the application is complete.

### 8.3 Responsive behavior

One frontend MUST adapt to ultrawide and standard monitors, laptops, tablets, and mobile devices. Separate device applications are not required.

### 8.4 Browser support

Current versions of Chrome, Microsoft Edge, Firefox, Opera, and Safari are supported. Legacy browser support is not required.

### 8.5 Reusability

Business logic for a reusable module, especially X-Ray, MUST NOT be permanently bound to one page or one application shell. Reuse SHOULD be achieved through explicit application and data contracts without prematurely requiring a complex microfrontend architecture.

### 8.6 Development and product separation

SRM is developed in GitHub Codespaces under the SRM project profile. The governing repository strategy remains one monorepo with separate applications or modules and independent deployments.

SRM and SpendGuru MUST NOT become one application merely because they share a repository. Shared packages, contracts, infrastructure, or provider integrations MAY be reused when ownership and tenant boundaries are explicit.

### 8.7 Continuity

Every governed SRM task uses the SRM PMOS profile and the dedicated srm_pmos database. PHR is required. MEMOROS is disabled by the registered SRM profile and MUST NOT be enabled or substituted through an environment variable.

## 9. Frozen delivery principles

The first useful SRM delivery SHOULD be small, demonstrable, and based on real data.

X-Ray is the preferred first vertical slice because it can demonstrate immediate supplier insight while establishing reusable foundations for Supplier Profile identity, provider integration, provenance, tenant boundaries, bilingual UI, and responsive presentation.

The first X-Ray demo direction includes:

- search by NIP or KRS;
- company name;
- current address;
- representation;
- financial data for all available provider periods, including revenue, costs, and profit or loss where available;
- beneficial owners;
- available Vercly compliance or list results;
- source and freshness metadata;
- a browser-based report view.

This list is a frozen demo direction, not a declaration that every provider field already exists or that a specific historical implementation is canonical.

## 10. Accepted sequencing assumptions

These assumptions govern later planning:

1. Establish supplier identity and source provenance before broad feature growth.
2. Establish controlled taxonomy before category-driven discovery, ownership, and onboarding.
3. Use governed imports before direct enterprise integration where this accelerates a safe first result.
4. Deliver the internal user flow before external supplier self-service unless a later decision changes priority.
5. Keep performance evaluation separate from procurement-process evaluation.
6. Treat contact chronology as the primary meaning of meeting map.
7. Treat X-Ray as a reusable capability within Supplier Profile and Supplier Intelligence, not as the whole SRM product.

## 14. Drift-control and change procedure

This v1.0 baseline is frozen.

A later task MAY clarify wording without changing meaning, but any change to product scope, capability ownership, constraints, or accepted sequencing MUST:

1. identify the exact affected section and capability IDs;
2. cite new business or technical evidence;
3. state whether the change is additive, corrective, superseding, or removing scope;
4. assess impact on architecture, repository placement, roadmap, integrations, and existing implementation;
5. use the SRM governance lifecycle, srm_pmos, and required PHR publication;
6. publish a new version or explicit amendment rather than silently rewriting history.

Chat history, meeting narration, a prototype, a local folder, a deployment name, or an implementation shortcut MUST NOT silently override this Canon.

If current code and this Canon differ, the difference MUST be reported as drift. It MUST NOT be resolved by assuming either side is correct without a governed decision.

## 15. Canonical freeze statement

As of version 1.0, the canonical SRM direction is:

- one trusted Supplier Profile;
- reusable X-Ray and Supplier Intelligence;
- governed identity, taxonomy, provenance, and tenant isolation;
- discovery, evaluation, cooperation history, agreements, relationships, onboarding, communication, and intelligence as bounded product capabilities;
- internal usefulness before deep automation;
- separate SRM product identity inside the wider monorepo ecosystem;
- architecture allocation and implementation roadmap to follow as separate governed artifacts.

This statement is authoritative until superseded by a later approved version.
