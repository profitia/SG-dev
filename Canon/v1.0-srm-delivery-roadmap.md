## 1. Purpose and authority

This document freezes the delivery roadmap for the first SRM X-Ray product increment. It translates the active SRM Business Capability Canon and SRM Architecture and Component Placement Canon into ordered, independently reviewable implementation stages.

This roadmap does not replace either parent Canon. If a roadmap statement conflicts with an active higher-order Canon, the higher-order Canon prevails and this document must be corrected through a reviewed governance change.

The roadmap is binding for implementation sequencing, stage entry and exit criteria, environment ownership, and the boundary between the first demonstrable product and later capabilities.

## 2. Frozen product decision

SRM X-Ray is one coherent user-facing module. A user enters a Polish NIP or KRS and receives one consolidated supplier card. Supplier Master is the internal identity and normalization foundation of that card; Registry, Compliance, Financial Data and future Financial Analysis are sections of the same X-Ray experience, not separate user-facing applications or disconnected delivery products.

The first complete flow shall consolidate:

1. company search by NIP or KRS;
2. supplier identity and general company data;
3. current address, representation and public registry facts from MGBI-supported sources;
4. all provider-approved financial periods available through MGBI;
5. KYC and compliance facts, beneficial owners and list results from Vercly;
6. source, retrieval time, freshness and section-level quality information;
7. a single reusable X-Ray report/card contract suitable for embedding in SRM surfaces.

Financial ratios, warnings and recommendation logic remain separate governed capabilities. They may be added only after their business definitions and decision policies have been explicitly approved.

## 3. Delivery principles

1. **One product card.** Registry, financial and compliance sections compose one X-Ray result.
2. **Server-side provider isolation.** Provider credentials, raw transport contracts and retries never enter browser code.
3. **Canonical normalized contract.** UI components consume SRM-owned types, not provider payloads.
4. **Provenance by default.** Every externally sourced fact carries source and observation metadata where available.
5. **Partial success is first-class.** One provider failure must not erase valid results from another provider.
6. **Tenant-aware from the start.** Supplier and report records are scoped to an explicit tenant context even in a single-demo environment.
7. **Reusable composition.** The X-Ray module must be embeddable within SRM without duplicating provider orchestration.
8. **No premature scoring.** Undefined financial indicators or compliance recommendation rules must not be fabricated.
9. **Separate product persistence.** SRM application data uses the SRM product database; PMOS history remains in srm_pmos.
10. **User-owned environments.** The user creates and recognizes Neon, Render and provider-side resources from explicit stage instructions.

## 4. First-demo scope

### 4.1 Included

- NIP and KRS search with validation and identifier normalization;
- supplier name and stable identity;
- NIP, KRS and available registry identifiers;
- current address and available branches or contact facts;
- representation and company web addresses when provided;
- financial periods available and approved for use from MGBI;
- revenue, costs and profit or loss for available periods when supplied;
- Vercly KYC/compliance checks and beneficial owners available under the approved contract;
- source, retrieved-at and freshness metadata;
- explicit loading, complete, partial, stale and error states per section;
- one responsive browser report/card;
- test doubles and deterministic demo fixtures;
- audit-friendly request correlation without storing provider secrets or prohibited raw data.

### 4.2 Excluded from the first demo

- automatic supplier approval or rejection;
- risk score or recommendation without approved policy;
- undefined financial indicators;
- alerting and periodic monitoring;
- bulk supplier import;
- SpendGuru workflow integration;
- production-scale multi-organization rollout;
- provider contract expansion not confirmed during the capability audit;
- client-specific technical identifiers.

## 6. Stage contracts

### Stage 0 — Roadmap freeze

**Objective:** establish this roadmap as the active delivery baseline.

**Deliverables:**

- this Canon document;
- active manifest registration;
- independent review through a pull request;
- successful SRM PMOS closeout and PHR publication for the governance task.

**Exit gate:** the roadmap is merged to main, discoverable through the active governance manifest and resolves through the Governance Source Layer.

**Prohibitions:** no application scaffold, provider secret, Render service, Neon product database or provider-side resource is created in this stage.

### Stage 1 — Provider capability and contract audit

**Objective:** determine exactly what may be reused from current MGBI, Vercly and historical implementations.

**Required evidence:**

- current canonical SG-dev assets and interfaces;
- read-only review of the historical company-intelligence runtime as history only;
- provider endpoint inventory, authentication model, response examples, limits and contractual data-use boundaries;
- field mapping to the frozen X-Ray scope;
- explicit gaps, unsupported fields and data-retention constraints.

**Exit gate:** approved capability matrix with each required field classified as available, derived, missing or deferred, and every reusable component classified as reuse, adapt or rebuild.

**Prohibitions:** the archive is never a development or deployment source.

### Stage 2 — Product environment registry

**Objective:** establish immutable target identities before any infrastructure mutation.

**Deliverables:**

- proposed names and identifiers for the SRM product Neon project/database and Render environment/service;
- GitHub repository and branch policy;
- environment-variable contract with secret/non-secret classification;
- separation proof between product persistence, srm_pmos and all SG2/CIC databases;
- rollback and cost notes.

**User responsibility:** the user creates or confirms external resources following step-by-step instructions. The implementation agent verifies identifiers before use.

**Exit gate:** reviewed target registry with exact resource identities and no ambiguous names.

### Stage 3 — Governance onboarding and application scaffold

**Objective:** create the lawful application surface without implementing provider behavior.

**Deliverables:**

- apps/srm application scaffold;
- ownership and routing coverage for every new path;
- baseline CI workflow where approved;
- package boundaries for X-Ray domain, application, provider adapters and UI;
- health endpoint and local/test configuration without secrets.

**Exit gate:** clean install, typecheck, lint, unit test and production build; routing guard passes for all new paths.

### Stage 4 — Tenant, supplier and product-data foundation

**Objective:** create the smallest persistent domain required by the X-Ray flow.

**Minimum concepts:** Tenant, Supplier, SupplierIdentifier, SupplierSnapshot, XRayRequest and XRayReport or their canonically reviewed equivalents.

**Constraints:**

- tenant identity is explicit on every persisted supplier/report aggregate;
- product migrations are committed and deployable through a repo-owned command;
- no PMOS tables or history are placed in the product database;
- no provider credential is persisted in application tables.

**Exit gate:** repeatable migration on an isolated database branch, database-to-schema consistency, tenant-isolation tests and empty production-target verification before first application write.

### Stage 5 — X-Ray shell and deterministic test doubles

**Objective:** validate the end-to-end user experience before live providers.

**Deliverables:**

- NIP/KRS input and validation;
- one responsive report/card with registry, finance, compliance and provenance sections;
- section-level loading, complete, partial, stale and error states;
- normalized SupplierXRayReport contract;
- deterministic fixtures for complete, partial and unavailable-provider cases;
- reusable embedding boundary.

**Exit gate:** approved browser flow on desktop and mobile-width layouts using test doubles only.

### Stage 6 — MGBI registry integration

**Objective:** supply lawful live company and registry data.

**Deliverables:** server-side MGBI client, timeout/retry/error classification, mapping to the normalized contract, source timestamps and contract tests based on sanitized fixtures.

**Exit gate:** NIP/KRS queries populate identity, address and representation without leaking credentials or raw provider coupling into UI code.

### Stage 7 — MGBI financial integration

**Objective:** add provider-approved historical financial data to the same report.

**Deliverables:** period normalization, revenue/cost/profit-or-loss mapping, currency/unit handling, missing-period behavior and provenance.

**Exit gate:** all available approved periods render consistently; missing or partial financial data does not invalidate registry results.

### Stage 8 — Vercly compliance integration

**Objective:** add KYC/compliance and beneficial-owner information to the same report.

**Deliverables:** server-side Vercly adapter, beneficial-owner normalization, list/check normalization, freshness and partial failure behavior.

**Exit gate:** compliance and beneficial-owner sections render from live approved data while other sections remain independently usable.

### Stage 9 — Consolidation, quality and partial-state hardening

**Objective:** make the composed result deterministic and operationally understandable.

**Deliverables:** orchestration, bounded parallel requests, correlation IDs, cache/freshness policy, data-quality flags, section-level observability and replay-safe normalization.

**Exit gate:** test matrix proves complete success, each single-provider failure, timeout, stale response, empty response and identifier mismatch behavior.

### Stage 10 — Staging and client demo readiness

**Objective:** provide a stable client-demonstrable system.

**Deliverables:** verified Render deployment, approved Neon product database, health checks, environment contract, sanitized demo suppliers, runbook and rollback plan.

**Exit gate:** a real browser flow from NIP/KRS search to one consolidated card is verified against GitHub commit, Render runtime/logs and relevant Neon schema/data. A successful build or HTTP 200 alone is insufficient.

### Stage 11 — Financial analysis

**Entry condition:** business owners approve indicator definitions, periods, units, missing-data policy and interpretation.

**Deliverables:** versioned indicator formulas, calculation tests, explanation and provenance for every result.

**Exit gate:** reference cases and business acceptance pass. This stage must not be inferred from raw provider data alone.

### Stage 12 — Recommendation and decision policy

**Entry condition:** explicit policy defines warnings, recommendation states, evidence thresholds, human accountability and appeal/override behavior.

**Deliverables:** versioned decision policy, explainable outputs, audit trail and safety tests.

**Exit gate:** governance and business approval. No recommendation is produced before this stage.

## 7. Cross-cutting gates

Every implementation stage must independently satisfy:

1. repository and workspace identity;
2. current main baseline and isolated branch/worktree safety;
3. lawful routing and ownership for all target paths;
4. code-state classification under the active architecture baseline;
5. SRM PMOS begin, save and closeout using only srm_pmos;
6. required PHR publication reachable from PHR main;
7. MEMOROS disabled by the SRM project profile;
8. no secrets, connection strings or client names in source-controlled technical identifiers;
9. tests proportionate to the changed surface;
10. live GitHub/Render/Neon verification whenever the stage changes those systems.

A dirty worktree or squash-history divergence is evidence to inspect, not automatic authorization to clean, reset or overwrite user work.

## 8. First-demo acceptance contract

The first demo is accepted only when a user can:

1. enter a valid NIP or KRS;
2. see the matched supplier identity;
3. see registry facts, current address and representation;
4. see available historical revenue, costs and profit or loss;
5. see available compliance checks and beneficial owners;
6. see the source and freshness of each section;
7. still use successful sections when another provider is unavailable;
8. view the result as one coherent, reusable X-Ray card.

The demo must not present an automatic recommendation, score or undefined financial ratio as an approved business conclusion.

## 9. Environment and responsibility boundary

The implementation agent prepares exact names, required settings, least-privilege access, validation steps and rollback instructions. The user creates or confirms Neon, Render and provider-side resources so their purpose and cost remain recognizable.

No stage may select an external resource by display name alone. Exact GitHub, Render and Neon identifiers must be recorded and verified before mutation.

## 10. Change control

This v1.0 roadmap is a frozen baseline. A change to stage order, first-demo scope, provider responsibility, persistence boundary, tenant model or recommendation entry conditions requires:

- a separate governance task;
- an explicit rationale and impact analysis;
- a reviewed Canon change through pull request;
- SRM PMOS closeout and PHR publication.

Implementation details may evolve within a stage only when they preserve the active SRM business and architecture Canons and the exit contract of that stage.

## 11. Authorized next step

After this roadmap is reviewed and merged to main, the next authorized delivery task is **Stage 1 — Provider capability and contract audit**. Stage 1 is read-only with respect to provider systems and the historical archive. It must finish before application scaffolding or product-environment creation begins.
